This fall I taught Queer American History. Every student in the class _wanted_ to be there, _wanted_ to do the readings, _wanted_ to talk about what they were learning -- it created a pretty magical learning environment!

One of the high points was a guest lecture by [Dr. Samantha Rosenthal](https://gsrosenthal.com/). With the help of the History Department and the Intercultural Center, I invited Dr. Samantha Rosenthal to join us on campus to talk about her book, [_Living Queer History: Remembrance and Belonging in a Southern City_](https://uncpress.org/9781469665801/living-queer-history/), published by UNC Press in 2021. The talk was wonderful -- several students wrote in their senior capstones that they found it inspirational. Follow [her substack](https://70014.substack.com/) if you're not already!

One of the greatest treats as a teacher is when students decide they want to do their senior capstone on something pertaining to what they learned in your class. This year, two of the seniors in my class told me they're now planning to write on queer history! One is even using the oral histories and digitized physical archive created by the [Southwest Virginia LGBTQ+ History Project](https://swvalgbtqhistory.org/) that Dr. Rosenthal helped lead.  

Huge thanks to Dr. Rosenthal and to Kathleen, David, and Gail who helped make this course so special!
